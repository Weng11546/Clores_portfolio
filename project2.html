Project overview

This is Case Study 9 from the Datamex College of Saint Adeline Data Analysis midterm. The workbook takes 50 raw post records (platform, content type, reach, likes, comments, shares, video length and campaign flag), cleans them, and turns them into descriptive statistics, platform and content-type comparisons, a platform × content-type summary, a top-5 ranking, and written recommendations.

Problem statement

A social media team needs to know where its effort pays off. Raw likes and reach numbers hide the answer: a post can be seen by many people and still be ignored. The data also contained entry errors that had to be fixed before any comparison could be trusted.

Objectives

  • Inspect and clean the raw data, including duplicate IDs and data-entry errors
  • Calculate total engagement and engagement rate for every post
  • Compare performance by platform and by content type
  • Test whether video length is related to engagement
  • Identify the top posts and give three or more evidence-based recommendations

Target users

  • Social media managers deciding where to publish and which formats to produce
  • Marketing teams who report on campaign performance
  • Course instructors assessing the analysis

Tools and techniques

Data cleaning

  • Duplicates: all 50 Post IDs (P9000–P9049) are unique, so no rows were removed.
  • Entry errors: 10 Text or Image posts had a video length above 0. These were set to 0 in a separate clean column, and the original values were kept for audit.
  • Missing durations: 16 of 19 videos have a length of 0, which looks like unrecorded data. These were flagged but not altered, because the true length cannot be inferred.
  • Range check: no negative or non-numeric values in reach, likes, comments, shares or minutes.

Derived metrics: Total Engagement = Likes + Comments + Shares. Engagement Rate = Total Engagement ÷ Reach × 100.

Key findings

  • Facebook is the most efficient platform: highest average reach and highest engagement rate.
  • Instagram has the lowest average reach (3,571) and the lowest average engagement per post (284), and its engagement rate (7.89%) trails Facebook.
  • TikTok is seen often but converts views to engagement least well (7.43%).
  • Video earns the highest engagement rate even though its reach is lowest.
  • The best combination is Facebook + Video (12.92%). The weakest is TikTok + Image (6.58%).
  • Video length could not be tied to engagement: the correlation with engagement rate is weak (−0.22) and rests on only 3 videos with a recorded length.

Recommendations

  1. Shift the mix toward video, especially on Facebook.Video has the best engagement rate, and Facebook + Video is the strongest pairing.
  2. Fix Instagram before posting more there.It has the lowest reach and engagement per post; audit formats, timing and hashtags first.
  3. Standardize data capture.Require Platform and Video_Minutes so future analysis can test video length properly.
  4. Judge content by engagement rate, not reach.Text leads on reach but is only average on engagement; reach alone would favor the wrong format.

Limitations

  • Only 50 posts, so the platform × format groups contain as few as 4 posts
  • 16 of 19 videos have no recorded length, so the duration test is inconclusive
  • The data has no dates, spend or audience details, so campaign effects are not isolated

Future improvements

  • Collect more posts, and record the real video length for each
  • Compare campaign and non-campaign posts
  • Build an interactive dashboard on top of the cleaned data
